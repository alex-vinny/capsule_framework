    Capsule — Business Model (Server Code Push as the Funding Engine)¶

    The framework is free and open source. The money is a managed cloud for OTA delivery + governance + analytics. This is open-core SaaS — the same model as Expo EAS, Appflow, and the (now-dead) CodePush. This doc captures the commercial strategy decided in the 2026-06-15 strategy session.

    Technical specs for the server itself live in ../server-design/overview.md (control plane), ../server-design/file-storage.md (bundle delivery/CDN), and ../../guide/publishing.md (codesync publish vs publish). This doc is the why and the who-pays, not the how.

    The model in one line¶

    Free, open, no-lock-in framework + Ionic/F7 migration on-ramp = the funnel. A managed "safe-deploy" OTA cloud whose flagship is canary + auto-rollback = the revenue. Sold first to agencies. Gated on iOS shipping.

    Why open source the framework at all¶

    Lock-in fear is the #1 reason developers refuse hybrid OTA tooling (Appflow is the cautionary tale). Open-sourcing the runtime, CLI, and sync protocol — and shipping a self-hostable server — makes the anti-lock-in pitch real and removes adoption friction. The cloud (convenience, scale, governance, analytics, multi-tenant) is the moat, not the protocol.

    This also defuses the CodePush death trap: CodePush offered unlimited free hosted OTA and the bandwidth bill helped kill it. With a self-hostable server, "free + unlimited" = bring your own infra — you're never on the hook for someone else's egress.

    Market timing (lead with this)¶

    • Microsoft App Center retired (March 2025); CodePush deprecated → many RN/Cordova/hybrid apps orphaned and actively shopping for a new OTA provider.
    • Appflow is expensive and Ionic-locked.
    • Wedge sentence: "CodePush is dead, Appflow is a tax. Capsule gives you OTA with automatic crash-rollback, an open-source runtime, and no lock-in."

    ICP — agencies first, freelancers as the funnel¶

    Three buyers want opposite things; do not design for all three at once:

    Buyer Wants Role in strategy
    Freelancers / indie devs Free + simple Funnel. Served by the free tier + OSS. Cheapest acquisition channel — they recommend Capsule into agencies. Keep them happy; don't design the product around them.
    Agencies (many client apps) Multi-tenant, many apps, per-client channels Primary ICP. Most apps, clearest multi-tenant pain, budget, most CodePush-orphaned. Land these first.
    Enterprises Canary, SSO, audit, on-prem, SLA Upmarket pull. Agencies pull you here; high-ticket, but heavier sales.

    Free vs paid — what's the line¶

    Meter on MAU + bandwidth, NOT on deploy count. Deploys are nearly free to serve and frequent small deploys are the behavior to encourage. The original "1 codesync/day / 100 test versions/day" axes were wrong — dropped. (Pricing table: pricing.md.)

    Free tier shape: unlimited production deploys, unmetered dev/staging channels, 1,000 MAU, 3 seats, 30-day / last-10-version retention, basic metrics. Plus a self-hostable OSS server with no caps (your infra). The premium modules (canary/auto-rollback, multi-tenant, advanced analytics, SSO, managed signing) are the open-core "closed" parts — absent from the free hosted tier and the OSS server.

    Paid feature priorities (graded)¶

    Feature Verdict
    Canary / pilot rollout + auto-rollback on crash signal 🥇 Flagship. Enterprises pay for safe deploys. The on-device crash-loop guard already exists (Phase 2 Step 7) — the server side reports crashes → halts rollout → auto-rolls-back. Monetizes work already done.
    Multi-app / multi-tenant per company 🥇 Enterprise gold. Exactly the agency + white-label-SaaS pain. Per-tenant channels + RBAC = top tier.
    Device segmentation (geo, version) ✅ Substrate canary/targeting rides on.
    Scheduled sync ✅ Easy paid lever.
    Metrics + logs portal + crash analytics ✅ Closes the loop with auto-rollback (data-driven decisions).
    Unlimited devices/deploys/seats ✅ Standard scaling lever.
    MCP server for AI ⚠️ Distribution channel, not a SKU. Make read/deploy-status MCP free (gets Capsule into every AI dev workflow); gate only scoped automation (CI deploys, agent-triggered prod rollout) behind paid.
    AI app-builder + online VSCode 🛑 Scope creep, wrong customer. Serves greenfield/new apps, not the migrate-off-Ionic ICP. A whole separate product (IDE infra, sandboxes, hosting). Defer, or run a stripped version purely as lead-gen — never a revenue line now.

    Features the original list missed (enterprises pay for these)¶

    1. Compatibility gating — minNativeVersion / bridge-contract version per bundle so a new OTA refuses to activate on an old shell before it crashes (already in publishing.md). Feature + safety story. See ota-policy-and-liability.md.
    2. Mandatory vs optional updates + forced-update screens.
    3. Differential / binary-diff updates — cuts your bandwidth bill and sells as "instant updates." Pure margin (see ../server-design/file-storage.md).
    4. Compliance tier — SSO/SAML, audit log, RBAC.
    5. On-prem / self-managed Enterprise license — banks/gov/health. High-ticket; it's the OSS server + license + support SLA.
    6. Bundle signing / integrity — tamper protection on the OTA payload.

    Store positioning (the superpower)¶

    Two update paths (full detail in ota-policy-and-liability.md and publishing.md):

    • capsule codesync publish → OTA, instant, no store review (most updates: JS/web bundle + QuickJS logic).
    • capsule publish → automated store submission via Fastlane/store APIs (native-shell changes only).

    Headline: most updates never touch the store. The store becomes an occasional event, not a per-release tax. Never market this as "bypass review" — see the liability doc.

    Open risks tracked¶

    1. iOS is the gate. Almost nobody pays for Android-only OTA. Monetization is sequenced after iOS. See roadmap re-sequencing in ../roadmap.md and bring-up plan in ../../contributing/ios-bring-up.md.
    2. Apple/Google OTA policy + the QuickJS-vs-JavaScriptCore clause → ota-policy-and-liability.md.
    3. Trust / reliability. A startup running production OTA infra must earn trust: uptime, SLA, CDN reliability. Infra economics in ../infrastructure.md.
